#### 27. Bhagavad Gita l Chapter 3 Verses 1-3 l Swami Sarvapriyananda
**Chapter Two (Sankhya Yoga):** Contains the core essence of the teaching; focuses specifically on the **Way of Knowledge**. **Chapter Three (Karma Yoga):** Focuses specifically on the **Way of Action**.**The Translator's Desire:** Reviewers note that simply reading the Gita often seizes the reader with an immediate desire to translate it.*The Hierarchy of Scriptural Commentaries Indian philosophical tradition categorizes text commentaries into three distinct levels of depth:* **Bhashya (Foundational Commentary)** A full-fledged, comprehensive commentary. The commentator is expected to establish a complete and rigorous system of philosophy. Himalayan monks of the Shankara lineage revere Shankaracharya as **Bhagavan Bhashyakara** (Our Lord, the Master Commentator). **Vartika (Clarifying Commentary)** A secondary commentary written directly on a *Bhashya*. Used to clarify, defend, or expand upon points that remained obscure in the original text.Shankaracharya wrote the *Bhashya* on the *Taittiriya Upanishad*, and his oldest, most learned disciple, **Sureshwaracharya**, wrote the corresponding *Vartika*. **Tika / Gloss / Annotation (Explanatory Notes)** Lower-level commentary focusing on local text elucidation. Specifically used to define and clarify difficult words and phrases. **Shridhara Swami’s commentary** (known as *Shridhara Tika* or *Subodhini Tika*). **Vartika** definition says, **uktānukta-duruktānāṁ cintā yatra pravartate |vākyaṁ tad vārttikaṁ prāhuḥ vārttika-jñā manīṣiṇaḥ ||**.((Parāśara Purāṇa)“Wise knowers of a vārttika say a vārttika (gloss) is a statement in which stated, unstated, and poorly stated thoughts [in a text] are brought forth.”). **Madhusudana Saraswati**, who was a contemporary of **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu**, Sri Chaitanya.**Gudharta Deepika**. The lamp of the profound inner meaning of the Bhagavad Gita.Before reading colored interpretations, read the original Bhagavad Gita to form your own understanding. However, you should also study commentaries by enlightened saints. While you will naturally interpret the text at your own current level of understanding, these profound reflections will greatly enrich your perspective. Selfless service (**Karma Yoga**) purifies the mind, making it ready for Vedantic study. This purity naturally yields the **Sadhan Chatushthay**, the fourfold qualifications required for effective enlightenment:**Viveka**: Discernment between the eternal and the temporary.**Vairagya**: Dispassion for the temporary.**Shat-sampatti**: Sixfold mental discipline:**Shama**: Control of the mind, **Dama**: Control of the senses, **Titiksha**: Spiritual fortitude, **Uparati**: Withdrawal from sensuous distractions, **Samadhana**: Focus, **Shraddha**: Faith in the teacher and scripture, **Mumukshuttvam**: Intense desire for liberation from the cycle of suffering (samsara).Developing these virtues makes the study of Vedanta effective, paving the direct path to enlightenment.**Chapters 1–6 (Tvam / Thou)**: Reveals the real nature of the individual soul (Jiva).**Chapters 7–12 (Tat / That)**: Reveals the real nature of God (Saguna Brahman).**Chapters 13–18 (Asi / Art)**: Connects them, establishing the ultimate identity between the individual and the Divine.**3.1 अर्जुन उवाच ।ज्यायसी चेत्कर्मणस्ते मता बुद्धिर्जनार्दन ।तत्किं कर्मणि घोरे मां नियोजयसि केशव ॥ 1॥ व्यामिश्रेणेव वाक्येन बुद्धिं मोहयसीव मे ।तदेकं वद निश्चित्य येन श्रेयोऽहमाप्नुयाम् ॥ 2॥** *(arjuna uvācha jyāyasī chet karmaṇas te matā buddhir janārdana।tat kiṁ karmaṇi ghore māṁ niyojayasi keśhava।। vyāmiśhreṇeva vākyena buddhiṁ mohayasīva me। tad ekaṁ vada niśhchitya yena śhreyo ’ham āpnuyām।।)* **Arjun said: O Janardan, if You consider knowledge superior to action, then why do You ask me to wage this terrible war? My intellect is bewildered by Your ambiguous advice. Please tell me decisively the one path by which I may attain the highest good.***Karma Yoga and Jnana Yoga are stages of a single spiritual journey. Karma Yoga acts like the ground transport (e.g., traveling from Asansol to Kolkata airport) that provides the necessary preparation, purifying the mind and building the fourfold qualifications (Sadhan Chatushthay).Jnana Yoga acts like the flight to Dublin, building on that preparation to deliver final enlightenment. As Madhusudana Saraswati noted, you cannot skip the preparation; Karma Yoga readies the mind, and Jnana Yoga delivers the result.If you would like, we can explore how Madhusudana Saraswati integrates the third path—Bhakti Yoga (devotion)—into this journey, or dive deeper into how to transition smoothly from action to knowledge.The battlefield of the Gita represents your daily life; you must face whatever challenges life presents. Many spiritual seekers rebel against Karma Yoga (selfless action), grumbling that they are already prepared for advanced meditation.However, trying to meditate without preparation results in becoming a "sleepy Buddha" because the mind lacks the capacity to stay still. Swami Prabhavanandaji notes that the Beatitude "Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God" highlights the absolute necessity of inner purity, which is achieved through Karma Yoga.To test your own mind's purity, try a simple exercise: stop right now and hold a single thought of the Divine (like Krishna, Christ, or Om). If your mind wanders within seconds, it reveals deep-seated impurities. Karma Yoga is the essential tool required to clear these distractions and steady the mind.*
